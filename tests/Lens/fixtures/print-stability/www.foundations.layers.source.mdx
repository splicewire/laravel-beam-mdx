# The layers

Beam, Schemastud and Splicewire are one family of packages written by one team. The three vendor
names are not three products competing for your attention; they are three layers of the same stack,
split by role. Once you know the roles, every package name in a `composer.json` tells you where it
sits and what it is allowed to know about.

```
your application            the host: your models, your policies, your routes
│
├─ splicewire/*             the product layer
│     laravel-beam …        Beam: the free SaaS application framework
│     engines & services    Splicewire's paid generation engines and hosted integrations
│
├─ schemastud/*             the schema substrate
│     laravel-data-schemas  a typed data class becomes a JSON Schema and TypeScript types
│     frame, seam           schema-driven resource screens, forms and widgets
│
└─ rushing/*                general-purpose foundations
      laravel-popcorn …     registries, capability dispatch, filters — no product opinion
```

- **`rushing/*`** is the foundation shelf: small, general-purpose packages that would make sense in
  any Laravel application. The one you will meet first is
  [`rushing/laravel-popcorn`](https://github.com/stephenr85/laravel-popcorn), which supplies the
  [registries](/beam/docs/foundations/registries) the rest of the family is organized around.
- **`schemastud/*`** is the schema substrate: it turns an ordinary typed PHP data class into a JSON
  Schema, generated TypeScript and schema-driven UI. It is independently usable — see
  [the Schemastud docs](https://schemastud.io/docs) — and it knows nothing about Beam or Splicewire.
- **`splicewire/*`** is the product layer. [Beam](/beam/docs/laravel) is the free, open SaaS
  framework built on the two layers below: accounts, tenancy, workflows, resource APIs, UI shells.
  Splicewire's paid engines and hosted services live beside Beam in the same vendor, as optional
  services a Beam application can connect to — never as prerequisites.

## The one rule

**Dependencies point downward only.** A `schemastud` package never mentions Beam; a `rushing`
package never mentions either; and no layer ever names your application's vocabulary. Your models,
your tenants and your domain words exist only in your own code, at the top.

This is why you can enter the stack at any layer. If you only want schemas and forms, take
`schemastud/*` and stop. If you are building a SaaS application, take Beam and it brings the layers
below along. If you later want Splicewire's paid services, they attach to what you already have.
[Install and compose Beam](/beam/docs/laravel/adoption) walks the practical combinations, and
[the package reference](/beam/docs/laravel/reference) names what each package owns.
